Our convictions

As part of the Institutional Investment Group, the core value proposition of ServiceInvest Kapitalverwaltungsgesellschaft mbH for its investors and employees is based on giving sustainability factors significant weight in all of its activities and services.

Driven by the market position we have achieved and the correspondingly greater social responsibility – but also by general developments in society – our principal aim is to use our own sphere of influence to make a meaningful corporate contribution to a sustainable environment and society.

For this reason, ServiceInvest has translated the general principles of sustainable corporate governance into its own company-specific sustainability strategy, which makes sustainable action within and through our company binding and also takes into account any adverse impacts of investment decisions on sustainability factors.

Key principles of our sustainable conduct

The key elements and principles of our conduct are:

  • Promoting responsible and sustainable conduct by our property management partners by embedding codes of conduct in the business relationship.
  • Promoting investment and property management initiatives for real estate that contribute positively to
    • reducing CO2 emissions and
    • lowering the overall cost burden on tenants through reduced heating costs and service charges.
  • Promoting the responsible use of resources.
  • Promoting information technology and processes designed to make
    • the consumption of resources by fund properties transparent and
    • sustainable action measurable.
  • Sanctioning conduct aimed at violating key sustainability principles.

Codes and memberships

The Institutional Investment Group, together with all of its subsidiaries, is a signatory to the United Nations-supported Principles for Responsible Investment (PRI) initiative. This network of international investors is committed to establishing the principles of responsible investment and putting them into practice. The PRI were developed by the investor community and reflect the view that environmental, social and governance (ESG) issues can affect the performance of investment portfolios and must therefore be given appropriate consideration by investors who wish to fulfil their responsibility to society. By implementing the principles, signatories contribute to the development of a more sustainable global financial system.

Signatory of

Launched in 2006 by the UNEP Finance Initiative and the UN Global Compact, the PRI provide a voluntary framework through which all investors can incorporate ESG issues into their decision-making and ownership practices and thereby better align their objectives with those of society at large.

The 6 Principles for Responsible Investment

  1. Principle 1:

    We will incorporate ESG issues into investment analysis and decision-making processes.

  2. Principle 2:

    We will be active owners and incorporate ESG issues into our ownership policies and practices.

  3. Principle 3:

    We will seek appropriate disclosure on ESG issues by the entities in which we invest.

  4. Principle 4:

    We will promote acceptance and implementation of the Principles within the investment industry.

  5. Principle 5:

    We will work together to enhance our effectiveness in implementing the Principles.

  6. Principle 6:

    We will each report on our activities and progress towards implementing the Principles.

UN Global Compact

The UN Global Compact is the world’s largest and most important initiative for responsible corporate governance. It is based on 10 universal principles in the areas of

  • human rights,
  • labour standards,
  • environmental protection and
  • corruption prevention

and, through the 17 Sustainable Development Goals, also pursues the vision of an inclusive and sustainable global economy for the benefit of all people, communities and markets.

The 17 Sustainable Development Goals are:

  6. Clean water and sanitation
  7. Affordable and clean energy
  8. Decent work and economic growth
  9. Industry, innovation and infrastructure
  10. Reduced inequalities
  11. Sustainable cities and communities
  12. Responsible consumption and production
  13. Climate action
  14. Life below water
  15. Life on land
  16. Peace, justice and strong institutions
  17. Partnerships for the goals

Consideration of sustainability risks in investment decisions

Sustainability risks are environmental, social or governance events or conditions that, if they occur, could cause an actual or a potential material negative impact on the value of an investment.

As part of our investment decision-making process, sustainability risks are identified and assessed in the course of our Adverse Sustainable Screening. In doing so, we examine an investment against defined ESG criteria for events or conditions that could have a material adverse effect on its value. The results of this screening are taken into account in the final investment decision. If the sustainability risks identified exceed the materiality thresholds we have set, this may lead to the investment being rejected on sustainability grounds.

Systematic consideration of adverse sustainability impacts

As part of our Adverse Sustainable Screening, investment proposals are specifically examined for principal adverse sustainability impacts and sustainability indicators:

  • Environment & biodiversity

    Indications of inadequate handling of compliance with environmental standards or of the avoidance or remediation of contamination

  • Gases contributing to the greenhouse effect without a phase-out scenario

    Indications of direct support for the production or distribution of ozone-depleting gases without a phase-out scenario

  • Controversial weapons / armaments

    Indications of direct support for the production or distribution of controversial weapons (cluster bombs, anti-personnel mines) or relevant system components

  • Human & labour rights

    Indications of inadequate handling of violations of international human rights conventions or the ILO core labour standards (child labour, forced labour, discrimination, freedom of assembly)

  • Prevention of money laundering

    Indications of inadequate handling in screening or of money laundering incidents

  • Prevention of corruption

    Indications of inadequate handling in screening or of corruption and bribery incidents

In our Principal Adverse Impact Statement, we focus on the following three indicators: (i) fossil fuels, (ii) energy efficiency and (iii) energy consumption intensity. Our policies on the identification and prioritisation of these sustainability indicators are described in the statement.

The results of the Adverse Sustainable Screening are taken into account in the final investment decision and, where materiality thresholds are exceeded, lead to our veto on sustainability grounds.

Sustainability in the remuneration policy

Since the company was founded, our sustainability convictions have been a central element of our remuneration policy. This applies in particular to employees with a variable remuneration component, as compliance with our sustainability convictions becomes part of their annual individual targets. In the event of sustainability breaches, variable remuneration components are not granted, to the extent permitted under employment law and depending on the significance of the breaches; to that extent, sustainability breaches are sanctioned financially. In addition, individual bonuses are in some cases agreed for achieving positively defined sustainability targets. In this way, we also wish to recognise meaningful sustainable conduct financially.
